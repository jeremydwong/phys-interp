Do dynamics models have physics neurons?

Two-link arm in MuJoCo · three models trained from scratch, one pretrained VLM · neuron-level analysis in the style of Cammarata, “Neural Networks Are Already Clean” · companion to report 1

Abstract

We trained small networks to predict the motion of a simulated two-link arm that swings under gravity and hits a floor and a wall. Every term of its equation of motion is logged, so each hidden unit can be compared against the true gravity torque, interaction torque, contact force, acceleration and state. We then ran the analyses from the circuits and “already clean” work: a one-job census, linear probes, dataset-example galleries with negative poles, feature visualization, event-aligned contact responses, ablation and steering, and replays across training.

The answers differ by quantity. State is everywhere, and velocity is built exactly where the architecture says it must be: a video model's single-frame encoder has none (probe R² 0.02) and its first temporal block creates it (0.58, rising to 0.90). Gravity torque units exist and match the analytic gravity map, but gravity is almost free here because it is a function of posture. When gravity's direction is hidden, a transformer infers it in about two frames and stores it relative to the upper arm, in a 2-D plane that it causally uses, not in head-direction-like cells. Shoulder torques get their own units; elbow torques mostly don't. Contact appears as a few impact units that fire on the frame before a collision and stay silent at near misses, while units that look like contact units are mostly proximity detectors. Acceleration is decodable but never lives in single units. It is distributed and nonlinear, and ablating candidate units does not remove it.

In a pixel model, training makes units about 4× cleaner than random features, as the essay predicts. But most of the clean units encode posture or stillness, and the physical quantities a dynamics model must compute are mostly distributed.

1Testbed and methods

1.1 An arm whose physics we can read off

A planar two-link arm (upper arm 0.50 m, 2.0 kg; forearm 0.45 m, 1.2 kg) hangs from a shoulder hinge in a vertical plane. A floor sits 0.86 m below the shoulder and a wall 0.68 m to the right; only the forearm can reach them. Episodes last 3 s at 50 Hz, driven by one of three controllers: passive swinging, sums of random sinusoidal torques, or PD reaching to random targets with no gravity compensation. MuJoCo integrates

$$M(q)\,\ddot q \;+\; c(q,\dot q) \;+\; g(q) \;=\; \tau_{\text{act}} \;+\; \tau_{\text{contact}} \;-\; b\,\dot q$$

and at every frame we log each term separately: the gravity torque $-g(q)$, the Coriolis/centripetal torque $-c$, the interaction torque each joint receives from the other joint's motion, $M_{12}\ddot q_{2}+c_1$ at the shoulder and $M_{21}\ddot q_{1}+c_2$ at the elbow, contact forces by surface and segment, and the energies. The decomposition closes to $1.5\times10^{-5}$ N·m. From these we build 32 target variables in 8 families (posture, velocity, acceleration, gravity, interaction, contact, applied torque, energy) that every analysis shares.

The architectures follow the readability lessons of report 1: residual streams, pre-LayerNorm, one wide nonlinearity per block, and separate spatial, temporal and channel mixing. In the video model this separation is also a built-in control. Its encoder sees one frame at a time, so any velocity it carries must come from the image alone.

1.3 The census and its controls

For each live unit (active on more than 1% of held-out frames) we fit its activation $a$ as a piecewise-constant function of each target $y_k$ over 30 quantile bins, which captures rectified and tuned responses that a correlation would miss:

$$R^2_{\text{bin}}(a, y_k) = 1-\frac{\operatorname{Var}\!\big(a-\bar a_{\,\text{bin}(y_k)}\big)}{\operatorname{Var}(a)},\qquad k^\star=\arg\max_k R^2_{\text{bin}}(a,y_k)$$

A unit is clean if $R^2_{\text{bin}}(a,y_{k^\star})\ge 0.5$, partial if it is between 0.25 and 0.5, and unexplained otherwise. It is a conjunction if its best two-variable fit beats its best single variable by more than 0.15. These thresholds are ours; the essay gives none. Three controls keep the census honest:

  • Random-init control. The same architecture at step 0. With an 8-dimensional input, a random unit is already a smooth function of a few angles, so “clean” means little unless it beats this.
  • Aliasing. Elbow gravity torque equals a constant times cos(q₁+q₂), and shoulder gravity torque is nearly linear in cos q₁ and cos(q₁+q₂). A “gravity unit” may be a posture unit.
  • State control (video model). Regress out an MLP's prediction of the unit from the true instantaneous state and redo the census on what is left.

2State MLP: a forward model on joint state

This model gets the arm's state and the motor torque and must output the next state. To do so it has to compute, implicitly, the gravity and Coriolis torques, the inverse mass matrix and any contact reaction. A linear model on the same inputs explains only 10% of the velocity change; the MLP explains 97%.

2.1 Census and probes

The census is largely negative. 21.3% of live units are clean, against 25.0% at random initialization, because random units of an 8-input network are already smooth functions of posture. Training changes what units are clean for: units cleanly tuned to a computed quantity (acceleration, gravity, interaction, contact, energy) rise from 4.6% to 7.5% overall and to 12.6% in block 2. Late blocks become harder to read, with 51% and 57% of units unexplained. Probes tell the same story from the other side: training adds most over random features for Coriolis (0.82 vs 0.59), contact (0.84 vs 0.70) and interaction torque (0.60–0.67 vs 0.50).

2.3 Gravity: receptive fields, feature visualization and causal tests

Evaluated over a grid of postures at rest, the shoulder-gravity units are rectified copies of the negative lobe of the analytic shoulder gravity map (grid R² 0.67 and 0.68). They are genuine “arm extended, gravity pulling down” detectors and not artifacts of velocity or contact. The elbow “gravity” units have no gravity-like field at rest (0.03, 0.10); their data R² comes from the forearm angle. Gradient ascent on the input produces superstimuli just past the edge of the data: a horizontal arm pushed into the wall, a forearm 11 cm inside the floor. Contact-related units extrapolate like penetration-depth detectors.

Is the gravity code used? We compared the model's change in predicted acceleration with the change expected if gravity were scaled by $s$, $\Delta\ddot q^{\ast}=(s-1)\,M(q)^{-1}\tau_g(q)$. Zeroing all 66 gravity-tuned units removes only 25% of gravity, and the damage is mostly not gravity-shaped. Writing $(s-1)\,\tau_g$ along the residual-stream direction that encodes gravity works better: after block 2 the effective gravity change is −0.82 at $s=0$ and +0.60 at $s=2$ (ideal −1 and +1). Writing along the probe's direction does nothing, even though that probe decodes gravity at R² 0.99. Decodable is not the same as used.

2.4 Segments, contact and acceleration

Segments. For each torque-tuned unit we computed a selectivity index $\mathrm{SI}=(R^2_{\text{shoulder}}-R^2_{\text{elbow}})/(R^2_{\text{shoulder}}+R^2_{\text{elbow}})$. Gravity units are 80% shoulder-specific and 0.6% elbow-specific. The shoulder gravity torque is a combination the network has to build; the elbow one is a single input-like feature it never needs to isolate. Coriolis units lean elbow (16%) but are mostly mixed, and contact-torque units are 97.5% mixed, as the physics predicts: one forearm contact produces torque at both joints.

Contact. Because the model predicts t → t+1, a unit that expects a bounce must fire on the last frame before contact. We selected units by how well they separate that frame from near misses (closest approach under 2 cm, no contact). The best, L4/49, sits at 0.01 on free frames and at near misses, jumps to 0.60–0.70 on the frame before fast floor and wall impacts, and is back at zero once contact begins: a collision-in-the-next-20-ms detector. No census target captures it (best R² 0.06). Units the census calls “floor contact” respond to proximity almost as much as to contact.

Ablating the 57 impact units removes about 21% of the predicted bounce while hurting free motion no more than ablating 57 random units. Ablating the census contact units barely touches the bounce but damages free motion. In an autoregressive rollout through a floor impact, ablating the union of these units does not make the arm pass through the floor; the post-impact dynamics diverge instead. Contact handling is distributed enough that ablation corrupts it rather than removing it.

Acceleration. We split the true acceleration into its causes, $\ddot q = M^{-1}(\tau_{\text{act}}-g-c+\tau_{\text{contact}}-b\dot q)$, and probed each part. On contact-free frames the step-mean acceleration the model is trained on rises from R² 0.32 at the input to 0.96 at block 4 (random init 0.75). Every component is separately decodable; training mainly buys the velocity-dependent Coriolis part, which carries 71–79% of the acceleration variance. The gravity and motor parts are equally decodable from random features. No unit is clean for acceleration.

3Video model: physics from pixels

The video model never sees a number. It gets four 64 px frames covering 60 ms and must draw the next two. It beats copying the last frame by 91% on moving-arm pixels, and does as well at contact onsets (90%), where the predicted arm stops at the surface instead of passing through it.

3.1 Training makes units cleaner, but mostly for posture and stillness

Here the essay's prediction holds: 24.6% of live units are clean against 6.5% at random initialization, about 4×. A random convnet on pixels is not a smooth function of a few angles, so cleanliness has to be learned. By family, the clean units are posture (476), energy (294), velocity (141), gravity (63), interaction/Coriolis (27), contact (5), acceleration (0) and applied torque (0).

The state control removes almost all of the rest. A small MLP on the true instantaneous state predicts the median unit at R² 0.78. Of 1,075 units with a label other than posture or velocity, only 13 keep a partial R² ≥ 0.25 once the state is regressed out, and no dynamics variable keeps more than 0.21. Units encode the present, not the predicted next frame: average R² peaks for targets at frame t (0.445) over t+1 (0.423).

3.2 Velocity is built where it has to be

This is the cleanest result of the study. The per-frame encoder cannot know velocity and does not: probe R² for q̇ is 0.02. The first block of temporal attention raises it to 0.58, and by the last block it reaches 0.90 for the shoulder and 0.63 for the elbow. The random-init space-time stage gets 0.01. Acceleration and the torques follow more weakly and nonlinearly: q̈ reaches 0.33 linearly but 0.68 with an MLP probe, which beats an MLP given the true instantaneous state (0.55) because the frames carry history. Coriolis torque, a product of velocities, is decodable only after a nonlinearity (linear 0.36–0.52, MLP 0.69–0.84).

3.4 Feature visualization: the microscope timeline, replayed

We optimized the four-frame input to drive single units under three settings modelled on the essay's 2009 / 2017 / 2026 comparison: raw pixels with plain gradient ascent; a decorrelated Fourier parameterization with small jitter; and strong transformation robustness (jitter, scale) with a palette prior we added that paints the background and two arm-coloured masks. Naive optimization reaches about 2.5× the dataset maximum but is adversarial: a 2-pixel shift loses 65–80% of it. In three of five units naive and Fourier optimization stay at zero, because a GELU unit that starts inactive gets almost no gradient. Only the robust setting drives all five units, at or above the dataset maximum, and it survives shifts. Its images are arm-coloured blobs near the right place, readable only next to the dataset examples.

3.5 Contact: proximity units, one impact unit, and a negative ablation

Encoder units that look like contact units respond about as strongly to near misses as to contacts; their AUC for “contact in the next frame” is at chance (0.50–0.53). They are proximity detectors. One space-time unit, tmp3.48, behaves like a floor-impact unit: it is already up at lag −1, before contact is visible in its input, peaks at onset, returns to baseline within two frames and ignores near misses. Its AUC (0.76) beats a logistic regression on the true clearance and approach speed (0.73). Mean-ablating the 32 best contact units changes the predictions by 5% and does not make the arm pass through the floor; it hurts less than ablating random units.

4Gravity vector extraction

Gravity points in a random direction θg in each episode, nothing in the input says which, and there are no obstacles, so the only evidence is the motion. This is the one setting where a physical quantity is truly hidden. A causal transformer trained only to predict the next state infers it.

4.1 Fast inference, stored in the body frame

At the first frame the model is exactly as bad as a control that sees only one frame. One more frame cuts its median error 130×, and after that it predicts within 1.6× of a model that is told θg. A linear probe for gravity relative to the upper arm, θg − q₁, reaches 29° at frame 2 and 11° at frame 64. World-frame θg is barely linearly decodable at any layer. An MLP probe recovers both (world 5.0°, body 3.2°): the world direction exists only as a product of the body-frame code with posture. The physics needs only gravity's direction relative to the arm, $\tau_g$ depends on θg − q₁ and q₂, and that is exactly what the network keeps linear, much like an otolith mounted on the upper arm.

4.2 No head-direction cells

Direction in a plane is an angle, so we looked for a head-direction-like population: units with bump tuning and preferred directions tiling the circle. There is none. Only 3 of 2,048 MLP units are tuned to any gravity angle at R² ≥ 0.3, all in layer 1 and all in the upper-arm frame. 84 are weakly tuned: 59 in the upper-arm frame, 25 in the forearm frame and none in the world frame. Tuning is very broad (median full width at half maximum 360°, rectified-cosine rather than bump). Preferred directions cluster where gravity is perpendicular to the upper arm, where shoulder gravity torque peaks. Beyond kinematics, gravity explains about 1% of a typical unit's variance.

4.3 The plane is causal

Rotating the whole scene (gravity and the shoulder angle together, an exact symmetry of the physics) leaves the body-frame readout within 1.6° and the residual stream 95–96% invariant; what does rotate is 99.8% a single 2-D plane. We held the arm still, released it with the motors off, and overwrote that body-frame plane at block 4 with the encoding of a different gravity direction θ′ = θg + δ. The model's arm falls toward θ′ for every offset up to 360°. Read back by an ideal observer, the rollout's gravity lands within 19.5° of θ′, the same as the unsteered noise floor (24°). A random plane gives 59°, and the plane that best predicts world-frame gravity does nothing (85°). As in the state MLP, the direction a probe finds is not necessarily the one the network reads.

4.4 Fooled exactly as far as the physics allows

A hidden constant joint torque can mimic a rotated gravity. We built one that makes a decoy posture an equilibrium and did not tell the model about it. The model reports the decoy direction with slope 1.00, the same as an ideal inverse-dynamics observer (1.01), and its predictions stay as accurate as normal for small swings. When the same torque is given as an input, it reports the true gravity (slope −0.06). Trained only at 9.81 m/s², it also predicts equally well for |g| from 4 to 16. Strength is not linearly decodable, but the length of the direction readout correlates with |g| at r = 0.80. Gravity looks like a 2-D vector whose angle is direction and whose length is strength.

6Through the lens of recursive generalization

Cammarata's account of messy neurons is recursive generalization: a unit starts on one thing and grows outward along shared features, so its strongest activations and its weaker ones are related but not identical. We looked for this in two ways: replaying units across training checkpoints, and splitting a unit's activations into bands.

  • Tuning arrives in a window and then mostly holds. In the video model the clean fraction is flat at 6% until about step 700, climbs to 22% by step 4,500 and plateaus at 24% from 8k, while the error keeps falling. Tracked units acquire their final tuning between 1k and 5k steps.
  • Units drift to neighbouring variables. tmp3.48 goes hand x → kinetic energy → contact torque. tmp3.406 goes sin q₁ → kinetic energy → shoulder velocity. In the state MLP, L2/834 starts as an elbow-angle unit and becomes a hand-height / floor-contact unit, and the gravity unit sharpens (R² 0.35 → 0.91) and then broadens slightly toward hand x. Each step is to a variable that shares something with the last: a forearm near the floor is both a posture and a contact; a still arm is both low energy and resting on the floor.
  • Strong and weak activations can mean different things. The shoulder-gravity unit's 50–90th percentile band is explained by gravity, but its top 1% is explained by elbow velocity and Coriolis torque. The video stillness unit's top bands are dominated by floor contact. The velocity unit L1/321 means the same thing in every band.

This is consistent with recursive generalization but does not test it. The essay's prediction is about locality: new members of a unit's receptive set should sit next to existing members even when they are far from where it started. We have not yet computed that statistic. It is the first item in section 8.

7Discussion

Where the essay's picture holds. In the pixel model, training makes units about 4× cleaner than random features, and the cleanest units are crisp and spatially local. Units appear exactly where the architecture lets a quantity exist: velocity only after temporal mixing, as the factorized design intended. Strong and weak activations often share a theme, and units drift between related variables during training, as recursive generalization describes.

Where it does not, yet. The quantities a dynamics model must compute (acceleration, interaction torques, contact forces) are almost never single-unit features. They are decodable, often only nonlinearly, and ablating the best-looking units rarely removes them. The most physically meaningful and causally effective representations we found are low-dimensional directions: the gravity-encoding direction in the state MLP and the 2-D body-frame plane in the gravity transformer. Two observations sharpen this. First, the direction a linear probe picks out is often not the one the network reads: editing along the probe does nothing in both models, while editing along the encoding direction steers. Second, aliasing is everywhere in a small physical system, and several “physics” units are posture or stillness units under another name.

Why these models may differ from ImageNet models. They are tiny (0.8–2.1 M parameters), trained on a single low-dimensional system, and some have many dead units. Superposition pressure is low, so “messy” here more likely means “distributed because the computation is smooth and low-dimensional” than “packed.” The essay's claim is about neurons in large, well-trained networks; the size sweep and the pretrained models in section 8 are the right test.

For a motor-control reader. The gravity result looks like a known biological answer: a body-centred estimate of down, as from otoliths on the head or limb, rather than an allocentric one, stored as a population vector rather than in labelled lines. The model is fooled by a gravity-mimicking torque exactly as far as an ideal observer would be, which is the network's version of the tilt–translation ambiguity. The shoulder-heavy segment code mirrors which torques are hard: the shoulder sees the sum of both links' gravity and the interaction from the elbow.

8Limitations and next steps

Limitations

  • One seed and one size per model. Unit identities and census fractions will vary across seeds.
  • Census choices are ours. Binned R² with 30 bins, thresholds 0.5 / 0.25, and a +0.15 conjunction margin. Binned R² rewards narrow peaks, which inflates velocity and energy counts with stillness units. The trained-vs-random comparison is more robust than the absolute numbers.
  • Aliased targets. Elbow gravity torque is the forearm angle; shoulder gravity is nearly cos q₁; potential energy is the heights.
  • Dead units. More than half the state MLP's units are dead even at a lowered learning rate.
  • Only the forearm can make contact. An upper-arm versus forearm contact split is not possible in this scene.
  • Causal tests are partial. No single-unit intervention behaves like an isolated gravity or contact channel; steering accuracy is limited by open-loop drift.

Next steps (running or queued on the CUDA machine)

  1. Finish the VLM study (section 5), including verbal versus probe accuracy.
  2. Size sweep for the video model (0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4× width) and several seeds: do units get cleaner with capacity, as the essay's 92k → 7M → 28M ConvNeXt series did?
  3. The locality test for recursive generalization across dense checkpoints.
  4. V-JEPA 2, a pretrained video world model, through the same census.
  5. A slot / interaction-network model, to test whether pairwise edge messages carry contact forces (Cranmer et al. 2020).
  6. Robot policies. No one has published a force, torque or contact probe on OpenVLA, Octo or π0; the logging used here carries over to LIBERO and SimplerEnv (see the Levine note).
